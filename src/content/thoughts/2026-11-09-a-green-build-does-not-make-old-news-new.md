A website can build successfully while giving visitors the wrong impression.

The [news archive](/news-and-updates) and [Radar](/radar) stopped publishing new daily entries in July. Their old headings still said “News & Updates” and “Today's picks”. The pages were reachable, but the words made a historical record look current.

## Keep the work, change the promise

We kept the original articles, product picks and publication dates. We changed the archive introductions and marked the individual entries as historical. The old links and claims have not been rechecked as current facts.

The site feed had the same problem. It listed old news and essays but missed newer work. It now includes dated [Feral creations](/feral), such as [SNARL](/feral/snarl), as well as field notes and the historical news archive. The Feral ledger records calendar dates, not publication times. The feed says so.

A subscription is useful only when it points to real output. It does not need a daily schedule.

## Make the useful things easy to reach

We put the [Domain & Outage Checker](/lab/outage-check) in the footer and search shortcuts. We also kept the artwork for [Off Register](/feral/off-register) on the homepage, with a link to the newest Feral creation.

That tool's [first release](https://github.com/valorifutures/softcat.ai/pull/280) passed source tests and deployment checks. Those checks do not prove every visitor's DNS provider will respond. The tool makes that limit clear.

## What this review cannot prove

The site build checks internal links, generated routes, metadata and local assets. It does not prove that an external source is still online or that every browser interaction works on every device.

We have not rewritten historical news to make it appear newly verified. We have not invented a fresh model-price snapshot. The [Pipeline](/pipeline) records the blocked 9 November price check and points to the actual workflow.

The standard is simple. If a page says something is current, we need evidence. If it is history, we should say so before anyone has to work it out.
